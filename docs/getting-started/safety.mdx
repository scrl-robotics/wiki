# Safety

Before we talk about robots, it's important to quickly discuss safety. It should go without saying that combat robots are inherently dangerous and can easily cause serious injury or even death if handled improperly. The community takes safety very seriously, and improper handling of combat robots is an easy way to not only hurt yourself but also get yourself banned from competitions or communities. So don't do it!

## Spinning weapons

**Spinning weapons should NEVER be powered on outside of an enclosed test-box**. If you want to test outside of an enclosure, make sure to unplug the motor or remove the weapon belt. This includes testing at low speeds, as well as plastic prototype weapons. These are dangerous too!

For all weapon types, make sure to include a **weapon locking mechanism** in your design that stops it from moving and cannot be removed easily by accident.

When loading into an arena or test box make sure to follow the proper steps (reverse when unloading):

1. Transmitter on
2. Place transmitter aside
3. Load robot in
4. Turn robot on
5. Remove weapon lock
6. Close the enclosure
7. Pick up transmitter and proceed

## Failsafes

Most competitions will require robots to have a proper **failsafe** set. If the robot loses connection with the transmitter, it should stop moving. Most transmitters/receivers have an easy way to set this. Make sure to check the instructions for your radio system and set your failsafe properly before testing or competing.

## Lithium-polymer batteries

The lithium-polymer batteries used by most combat robots can be dangerous if handled improperly. Make sure to avoid over-discharging them, and always use a high-quality smart charger designed specifically for LiPo batteries. Charging should always be done supervised and in a fireproof container like a LiPo bag or ammo can, and batteries should always be stored at storage charge in such a container.

Proper safety procedures and considerations are described in more detail in the [Testing & Safety](/testing-and-safety/testing-and-safety) section of this guide.
