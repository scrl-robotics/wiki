# Rules

Beetleweight events follow the same rules as the majority of combat robot events. In North America, most events follow the [SPARC ruleset](https://sparc.tools/) which is summarized below.

## Building Guidelines

Unlike many other types of competitive robots, combat robots are allowed a lot of design freedom, which is one of the best parts about the sport! However, there are some rules that must be followed:

- No weapons or devices that would make a fight boring (nets, radio interference, EMPs, etc.) - this is a spectator sport and we want to see robots explode!
- No weapons or devices that would be harmful to nearby people (lasers, toxic or radioactive material, explosives/guns, etc.)
- No material that would be a pain to clean up (food & liquids, certain foams, glitter, etc.)
- Flamethrowers, projectile weapons, and excessively fast spinning weapons may or may not be allowed on an event-to-event basis so make sure to check with your local tournament organizers.

## Match Rules

Matches are generally **3 minutes** long, though some events may run shorter fights. A winner is declared when its opponent is unable to move for a **10-second countout**, after which it is Knocked Out.

If both robots are moving at the end of the match, a panel of judges will decide the winner based on the criteria of **Damage**, **Aggression**, and **Control**. Matches may also go to the judges in other rare situations, such as when both robots are knocked out simultaneously or when both robots are stuck together inseparably.

- Getting stuck on parts of the arena or debris generally counts as being unable to move, though some events may allow one "unstick" intervention per robot per match.
- Robots are allowed to pin or grab onto their opponent only for a limited amount of time, usually **10 seconds**, after which they must release. Holding onto or pinning an opponent does not count as them being unable to move for purposes of a KO.

## Weight Bonuses

The main design restriction for beetleweights is the weight limit of **3lbs**. However, there are a few ways to gain an additional weight allowance:

| Type | Weight Limit | Description |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| Standard | 3 lbs | Regular beetleweight |
| Shuffler | 3.75 lbs | Robots driven by feet powered by continuously rotating cams are considered Shufflers |
| True Walker | 6 lbs | Robots that walk with legs. Each leg must have at least 2 independent degrees of freedom, and there must not be any rolling elements in contact with the floor |
| Non-Traditional Locomotion (NTL) | 4.5 lbs | Any robot that uses means of locomotion other than wheels, shufflers, or true walkers. Often these robots harness the forces of their weapons in some way to move around. They also cannot have rolling elements in contact with the floor |
| Multibots (NHRL only) | up to 4 lbs each | Robots with multiple independent segments are allocated additional weight. Each segment may weigh up to 4lbs divided by the number of segments, up to a maximum of 4 segments |
