# Appendix II: Bending Titanium

Bending titanium is necessary to create an effective anti-horizontal wedge. However, due to its strength, titanium is difficult to bend using conventional methods.

It can be cold-bent in a press brake, but requires a **very large bend radius** and a great level of care to avoid cracking, so this method is not recommended unless you are very familiar with sheet metal and have access to appropriate equipment.

The much more common DIY method is to use a **propane torch** to heat the titanium, then bend it by hand. The heat softens it which allows it to be bent easily without cracking.

## Setup & Equipment

![Bending titanium setup](/img/wiki/appendices/bending-titanium-setup.png)

1. **A propane torch.** A small propane canister and a nozzle will cost about $50 at your local hardware store.
   :::danger
   Torches are extremely dangerous – only use it outdoors, away from any flammable objects, and keep a fire extinguisher or bucket of water handy.
   :::
2. **A vise** to hold the titanium piece as you bend it. If you don't have a vise, you can instead use a metal clamp to clamp the piece to a heavy non-flammable object such as a cinder block or large brick. For small pieces, you can even just simply grip it with a pair of locking pliers.
3. **A large pair of pliers** to grab onto and bend the other end of the piece after heating. Optionally, you can use a metal rod or tube to control the bend radius.

Start by marking out the desired **bend line**, then clamping the part in the vise such that there is a small amount of distance between the bend line and the vise. This allows the bend to center around your bend line, due to the bend radius.

## Bending Process

1. Begin heating the titanium with the torch, while being careful to point the flame at the titanium part rather than the vise.
2. Make sure the **entire length of the bend line is heated to a glowing orange** – longer bends will take more heat and are more difficult to bend correctly.
3. Once the entire bend length is orange, slowly bend the titanium over with a large pair of pliers.
4. Keep in mind that titanium **springs back**, so you need to bend it further than your intended angle. If it's a large bend, you may need to re-heat it a few times before it can be bent far enough.
5. Once you're done bending, carefully remove the titanium from the vise (keep in mind the vise itself is likely very hot too) and dunk it in some cold water to cool it down.

:::note
This method is not at all suitable if precise bends are needed, but most wedge designs do not require precise bends. It is advised to avoid designing titanium parts that require precise bends.
:::
