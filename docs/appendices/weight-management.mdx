# Appendix VII: Weight Management

Let's say you've designed your robot, have finally received all your parts, and just finished putting everything together. Everything works properly – just have to double check your weight… and you're **overweight**. Oh no!

This is a pretty common occurrence for beginner and experienced builders alike. It can be stressful to realize your robot is overweight but fear not! Before you start drilling holes in all your parts, there are a few easier ways to cut down on weight that you might want to try first.

## Measuring Weight

The best way to fix being overweight is to **not be overweight in the first place**. Planning the weight of the robot when it's made of so many different components can be difficult, but here are some tips to accurately estimate the weight of your robot before actually building it:

- **Solid parts** are very easy to estimate – simply assign the appropriate material to your part in CAD and it should tell you how much the part weighs.
- **For 3D-printed parts**, load the part into your slicer and slice it with your desired settings. The slicer will give you an estimate of the part's weight.
  :::note
  With default settings, slicers will almost always **overestimate** the weight of parts. You can fine-tune this to give accurate weights by printing a (ideally large) part and **reducing the material density in the slicer** proportionally to the difference in estimated and actual weight of the part.
  :::
- **For off-the-shelf components** like motors and batteries, most sellers will list the part's weight on their website, making them easy to estimate as well.
- **Weights for miscellaneous items** like screws and wires are hard to estimate, so it's usually a good idea to simply leave a general **weight allowance** for these. Make sure to leave a good amount, as they can add a lot of weight.

## Reducing Weight

Here are some effective ways to reduce weight that are easy and have minimal impact on the robot's functionality:

1. **Reducing weight of 3D-printed parts:** printed parts are cheap to manufacture and highly configurable – it's cheap and easy to simply print parts using lighter settings.
2. **Reducing/removing side and back armor:** removing armor from areas that are less likely to get hit can reduce weight while having minimal impact on the robot's functionality, so long as you're able to keep your front pointed at the opponent.
3. **Shorten wiring:** wires are made of copper which is very heavy, so shortening your wires can actually save quite a bit of weight.
4. **Remove hardware:** if you're only a few grams overweight, removing a few unimportant screws can sometimes shave off enough weight.
5. **Cutting/drilling holes:** only do this if all other options are exhausted. Start with the heaviest and most solid parts and remove material slowly to avoid harming structural integrity.
