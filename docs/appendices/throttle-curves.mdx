# Appendix VI: Throttle Curves & Expo

One of the biggest challenges beginners face has nothing to do with building the robot itself: **driving**. Combat robots are quite fast and zippy which can make them difficult to control for the inexperienced. Driving plays a role in success as big as if not bigger than the actual robot's design, so if you want to win you have to drive well.

While driving skill certainly comes mostly from experience, there are some things you can do to make it a little bit easier your first time around. One of these things is **tuning throttle curves** on your transmitter.

The output signal that your receiver sends to the ESC is a function of the input on the transmitter's sticks. By default, it's a simple **linear function** where the output perfectly mirrors the input. However, this function can be modified in a few ways that can make driving easier.

## Throttle Limiting

If you feel like your robot drives or turns too quickly in general, or if you're running a drive system with excessive speed such as some tangent or gear-drive setups, **throttle limiting** might be a good choice.

By setting input weight to a value below 100, a multiplier is applied to your drive inputs, which effectively **reduces the maximum speed of your drive motors** at all input levels.

Generally, throttle limiting affects the inputs before mixing. Thus:
- Reducing the weight of **Channel 1** will limit your forwards and backwards speed
- Reducing the weight of **Channel 2** will limit your turning speed

Most builders will opt to limit turning speed to be able to point the robot more accurately.

**On the FS-i6:** Throttle limiting can be adjusted in the **Functions Setup → Dual Rate/Exp** menu and adjusting the "Rate" value.

**On EdgeTX radios** (Pocket/Zorro): Throttle limiting can be adjusted by changing the weight values of channels on the **"Inputs"** menu.

## Expo Curves

Enabling **expo curves** makes the output function an exponential curve instead of linear. This allows you to fine-tune the sensitivity of the throttle throughout the input range.

For example:
- This can allow greater control at low throttle but greater speed at high throttle.
- Another application would be increasing power at low throttle, to help lower-torque drive systems get going.

- **Positive exponential curves** will increase low-speed sensitivity
- **Negative exponential curves** will decrease low-speed sensitivity

It's a good idea to play with your expo curves to figure out what feels best to drive with.

**On the FS-i6:** Expo curves can be adjusted in the **Functions Setup → Dual Rate/Exp** menu and adjusting the "Exp" value.

**On EdgeTX radios** (Pocket/Zorro): You can add an expo curve by entering the menu for the input to be adjusted and modifying the "Expo" value.
