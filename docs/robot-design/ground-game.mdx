# Ground Game

One of the most important factors to winning a match in combat robotics is the ability to **get under your opponent**. Being underneath your opponent is highly advantageous as it allows you to break their traction against the floor and push them around freely. Additionally, most weapon types require you to be under the opponent to work properly. As such, when underneath your opponent you are not only enabling your weaponry, but rendering theirs ineffective.

**Ground game** refers to mechanisms used to get underneath the opponent. They are most often integrated into robots' front armor packages and are extremely important in dictating the winner of head-on collisions. As such, many builders have gone to great lengths to optimize their ground game which has resulted in a plethora of unique designs floating around. This document will only go into ground game at a basic level.

## Wedges

The wedge is the oldest and most basic form of ground game. Consisting of a simple sloped face on the front of the robot, wedges are easy to manufacture, durable, and do a decent job of "wedging" under the opponent. At least, they used to.

![Wedge](/img/wiki/ground-game/wedge-1.png)

*A simple wedge: durable and easy to manufacture.*

Wedges were very popular in the early days of combat robotics, but as the meta evolved, wedges became less and less effective as a ground game mechanism. Due to their large contact area with the floor making it difficult to keep the entire wedge flush, narrower ground game elements like wedgelets and forks can slide under wedges easily. Wedges are still a common element of combat robots, as many run sloped front ends that could technically be considered wedges. However, they should not be relied on as a sole means of getting under the opponent.

However, wedges still serve one very important purpose in modern combat robotics: **countering horizontal spinners**. By armoring the front of your robot with a hard, flat surface that's angled upwards, horizontal spinners will struggle to bite and deal damage and instead be deflected upwards. And because most horizontal spinners don't run ground game mechanisms that may counter wedges, the downsides of a wedge are not relevant. Wedge design is discussed in the [Materials](/robot-design/materials) section.

![Anti-horizontal wedge](/img/wiki/ground-game/wedge-2.png)

*Anti-horizontal wedge armor angled to deflect spinners upwards.*

## Forks

**Forks** refer to long, narrow prongs that extend forward from a robot's front end and ride along the floor. Due to their narrow contact patch, it is very easy to keep them flush with the floor and thus they are extremely good at getting under opponents. Forks are the most common type of ground game in the modern day and are one of the most important tools in a builder's arsenal.

Forks are extremely versatile and can serve many functions besides simply getting under the opponent. Although there are many types of forks, based on observations they can generally be split into three main categories:

![Types of forks](/img/wiki/ground-game/fork-overview.png)

*From left to right: sharp, reach, and corralling forks.*

### "Sharp" Forks
Are small, short, and pointy forks featured mostly on vertical spinners. Their main purpose is to guide opponents into the robot's weapon at an optimal angle, as well as ensure that wedges cannot get underneath them. Because they are short, they are lightweight and durable but can easily get out-reached by robots with longer forks. As such, they are most commonly seen on robots with weapons that inherently counter long forks such as drum and eggbeater spinners, or on fast, drive-centric robots that aim to attack the sides or back of opponents that aren't defended by longer forks.

### "Reach" Forks
Are very long forks optimized for the sole purpose of getting under and controlling the opponent. One major weakness of forks is that they don't cover the entire front end of the robot. Thus, if you have longer forks than your opponent there's a good chance that on head-on engagements your forks will slide into the gaps between your opponent's forks and lift them off the floor before they have a chance to come into effect. While arguably the most effective means of getting under an opponent, the length of reach forks make them heavy and also vulnerable to attacks from the side. They are most often seen on control-oriented robots such as lifters and overhead attackers, but are also common on other bot types.

![Reach forks](/img/wiki/ground-game/reach-fork.png)

*Long reach forks for getting under and controlling the opponent.*

### "Corralling" Forks
Are similar to reach forks in that they are long, but their main purpose is not to get underneath the opponent. Often mounted to the front corners of robots, they serve to prevent opponents from escaping pins as well as making it difficult for them to get around to the robot's sides or attack its front corners. They can also help stabilize robots by increasing their footprint. Corralling forks are often made out of soft plastic rather than metal to minimize weight and maximize durability, which is important as their positioning often means they get hit often. Corralling forks are common on wide-bodied control-oriented robots and vertical spinners.

![Corralling forks](/img/wiki/ground-game/corralling-fork.png)

*Corralling forks prevent opponents from escaping pins.*

## Wedgelets

Wedgelets are a less common form of ground game that serves as a middle ground between forks and a full wedge, and can take the form of extra-wide forks or angled plates that cover the front of the robot. They are more durable than forks and can hold up to off-plane hits better based on geometry but can struggle to get under real forks. They are used often in similar situations as the short, sharp forks mentioned above. Well-designed wedgelets also have the advantage of covering more area than forks, making it more difficult for longer forks to slide into any gaps.

![Wedgelets](/img/wiki/ground-game/wedgelet-1.png)

*Wedgelets combine the durability of a wedge with the ability to get under opponents.*
