# Robot Archetypes

While there are an infinite number of ways to build a combat robot, several archetypes have emerged as popular due to their effectiveness. This is by no means a comprehensive list as countless more types of robots have been built and been successful, but it should be a decent starting point to get some ideas.

## Wedge Bots

The humble wedge bot is the simplest combat robot one can build, consisting only of a drivetrain and front armor to tank opponents' hits and push them around. Wedge bots are an excellent choice for beginners as they are easy to build and safe to test, and can be highly competitive if driven well.

![Wedge bot](/img/wiki/archetypes/wedge-bot-1.png)

Because they do not have to dedicate weight to a weapon system, wedge bots generally rely on thick armor, a powerful drivetrain, and strong ground game ([discussed in detail later](/robot-design/ground-game)) to control fights. They excel against opponents with slow drive or poor stability, and benefit especially from the use of downforce magnets at events with steel floors.

![Wedge bot](/img/wiki/archetypes/wedge-bot-2.png)

## Lifters

Lifters are the next step up from wedge bots - they utilize an articulated arm to lift or tip over opponents, allowing them a degree of control over the opponent that wedge bots lack. Lifters come in many shapes and sizes - some are wide and use a short lifter to maximize ability to control the opponent, while others use a longer arm to achieve more of a tossing motion. Some lifters also use an additional arm to grab onto the opponent and lift them entirely off the floor - these are often called **grapplers** or **suplexers**.

Lifters share many of the same strengths and weaknesses as wedge bots, but their additional flexibility and ability to control the opponent makes them more competitive.

## Vertical Spinners

Vertical spinners (**verts**) are currently the most common weapon type, and are generally considered the most competitive. They feature a spinning mass with one or more teeth, spinning upward perpendicular to the floor. Because they spin upward, they are able to brace against the floor when hitting an opponent, allowing them to stay planted while transferring maximum energy into the opponent and sending them flying.

![Vertical spinner](/img/wiki/archetypes/vertical-spinner-1.png)

![Vertical spinner](/img/wiki/archetypes/vertical-spinner-2.png)

![Vertical spinner](/img/wiki/archetypes/vertical-spinner-3.png)

![Vertical spinner](/img/wiki/archetypes/vertical-spinner-4.png)

![Vertical spinner](/img/wiki/archetypes/vertical-spinner-5.png)

Three main subtypes exist:

### Drum Spinners
Utilize a wide, low-diameter drum that generally covers the majority of the robot's front. The wide drum provides a more generous area of attack, and doubles as strong armor for the robot's front. Additionally, due to their smaller diameter, it is easier to make the robot very compact, allowing for more weight allocated to armor or a heavier weapon.

![Drum spinner](/img/wiki/archetypes/drum-spinner-1.png)

![Drum spinner](/img/wiki/archetypes/drum-spinner-2.png)

### Eggbeater Spinners
Also known as **beater bars** or simply **beaters**, are a middle ground between regular vertical spinners and drum spinners. They utilize a rectangular "beater" that provides a good balance of weight, area of attack, energy storage, and [bite](/drive-systems/weapon-design).

![Eggbeater spinner](/img/wiki/archetypes/eggbeater-spinner-1.png)

### Large-Diameter Vertical Spinners
Also known as **tower verts** or **reach verts**, utilize a very large diameter spinner. These robots often have a reach advantage against opponents, and the large-diameter weapon provides a large amount of bite, allowing them to hit incredibly hard with a relatively thin and lightweight weapon.

![Tower vert](/img/wiki/archetypes/tower-vert-1.png)

![Tower vert](/img/wiki/archetypes/tower-vert-2.png)

## Horizontal Spinners

Horizontal spinners have a blade that spins horizontally, parallel to the floor. They are also very common, and are very competitive when built well. Compared to vertical spinners, horizontal spinners generally run larger weapons and thus benefit from greater reach and a larger area of attack. However, since they cannot brace against the floor, they often go flying opposite the opponent when landing hits which can put them in a disadvantageous situation.

![Horizontal spinner](/img/wiki/archetypes/horizontal-spinner-1.png)

Horizontal spinners can be divided into three main categories:

### Midcutters
Have the blade mounted between the top and bottom of the chassis, often with arms that extend from the top and bottom panels of the robot. Since they hit at mid-chassis-height, they are the most effective at doing direct damage to the opponent's chassis and wheels. They also have the most reach, as the weapon is entirely disjointed from the chassis.

![Midcutter](/img/wiki/archetypes/midcutter-1.png)

### Undercutters
Have the blade mounted below the chassis, close to the floor. This allows them to poke underneath opponents, attacking their wheels and underbelly. While they sometimes struggle to do direct damage to the opponent's chassis, the lowness of the blade makes it difficult for opponents to get under them and push them around. Vertical spinners also struggle to hit the low blade compared to midcutters. As such, undercutters are generally considered the most competitive type of horizontal spinner.

![Undercutter](/img/wiki/archetypes/undercutter-1.png)

![Undercutter](/img/wiki/archetypes/undercutter-2.png)

![Undercutter](/img/wiki/archetypes/undercutter-3.png)

### Overhead Spinners
Have the blade mounted above the chassis. This allows them to run larger weapons, and provides defense against overhead attacks. Additionally, they tend to have better weight distribution allowing them to drive better. Unfortunately, the height of the blade often means they are vulnerable to vertical spinners. Additionally, they are unstable and struggle to drive upside-down, making them unreliable.

![Overhead spinner](/img/wiki/archetypes/overhead-spinner-1.png)

## Full-Body Spinners

Full-body spinners have a horizontally spinning weapon that surrounds the entirety of the robot. This gives them 360 degrees of attack area, in addition to allowing them to run very heavy weapons. While incredibly powerful in some circumstances, full-body spinners are difficult to design and can often be unreliable.

![Full-body spinner](/img/wiki/archetypes/full-body-spinner-1.png)

- **Shell Spinners** feature a spinning shell that covers the top and outside diameter of the robot. In addition to being a powerful weapon, the shell functions as armor covering the entire robot. Shell spinners run some of the heaviest weapons of any robot archetype, which allows them to hit incredibly hard but comes with the downside of a long spin-up time. Shell spinners also cannot drive upside down.

  ![Shell spinner](/img/wiki/archetypes/shell-spinner-1.png)

- **Ring Spinners** feature a spinning ring that surrounds the robot, but does not cover the top like a shell spinner. This allows them to drive upside down. The main downside of ring spinners is their complexity - they often rely on a complex system of bearings to constrain the ring which can be fragile and add a lot of friction.

  ![Ring spinner](/img/wiki/archetypes/ring-spinner-1.png)

  ![Ring spinner](/img/wiki/archetypes/ring-spinner-2.png)

- **Meltybrain Spinners** are an incredibly unique robot archetype - they use one or two wheels to spin the entire robot at high speeds. By using an onboard sensor to measure the robot's RPM, the drive motors can be pulsed once per rotation to move the robot in a given direction while spinning. Meltybrains are incredibly chaotic and fun to fight, and are also a great challenge if you want to do some coding!

  ![Meltybrain](/img/wiki/archetypes/meltybrain-1.png)

  ![Meltybrain](/img/wiki/archetypes/meltybrain-2.png)

## Overhead Attack Robots

Overhead attack robots have a weapon attached to the end of an overhead arm that they can swing into the top of an opponent to directly attack their top armor. These robots are very competitive as they have the highest chance of directly damaging an opponent's internals - most robots don't have a lot of top armor. Most commonly, a small vertical spinner is attached to the arm - these weapons are called **"hammer-saws"**. Cutting saws or drills can also be used to good effect.

---

There are many, many more types of robots - this is only an overview of the most common ones. If you would like to design a more exotic archetype, or come up with your own, please do! A lot of unique designs end up being the most successful.
